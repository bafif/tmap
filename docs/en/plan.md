# Project plan

[Español](../es/plan.md)

TellMAP is an interactive historical atlas for studying history from the sources. This document explains how it's
built, why the technical decisions were made, and what comes next.

## The idea in one sentence

A world map with a timeline from 3400 BC to today, where every place opens up its history (who ruled it, who lived
there, what happened) and, above all, its **sources**: what was written there in each era and what was written later
about that place and that era.

## Technical decisions

### Engine: Godot 4

Three paths were evaluated:

| Option | For | Against |
|---|---|---|
| **Godot 4** (chosen) | Free (MIT), lightweight, quality 2D and 3D, custom shaders, exports to desktop and web | Fewer ready-made GIS tools than the web |
| Web (MapLibre, deck.gl) | Huge GIS ecosystem, shareable with a link | Achieving the look of a strategy game (water, relief, particles, 3D) takes much more work |
| Unity or Unreal | Cutting-edge graphics | Heavy, commercial licenses, far more than needed |

Godot is enough for the visual quality we're after (shaders, particles, water, 3D) and keeps the whole project free.
The bottleneck isn't the engine but the data, and that's prepared with Python outside Godot. If a web version is ever
needed, Godot exports to WebAssembly; for something lighter, the same data pipeline could be reused with a web
viewer.

### The map technique

It's the technique used by grand strategy games, adapted to 5,400 years:

1. **Units.** The world is divided into ~58,000 units (like the provinces in those games). A texture stores, for
   each pixel, the number of its unit. The units aren't a grid: they're generated by cutting organic cells along
   Cliopatria's real borders, so within a unit the political history is the same.
2. **History as events.** For each unit, the pipeline computes its changes of owner (year, unit, before, after):
   ~1 million events in total. Moving the timeline applies those events forward or backward; for long jumps it
   starts from one of the 43 full "snapshots" saved every ~20,000 events.
3. **A small table per year.** The state of a year is a 256 × 227 pixel texture (one value per unit). The shader
   crosses the unit texture with that table and with a color palette. Changing the year only updates that table: no
   polygon is redrawn.
4. **The shader** (`shaders/mapa.gdshader`) does the rest: terrain color, relief shading, rivers, coasts smoothed with
   a distance field, nautical chart lines on the water, political color that fades as you zoom in, antialiased
   borders, inner border glow, selection, hover highlight and paper grain.
5. **Curved names.** For each Cliopatria record, the pipeline computes the main axis and curvature of its territory;
   the app draws the name letter by letter along that curve, with a distance-field font that scales without losing
   sharpness.

### Projection

Miller cylindrical, cropped between 83° N and 57° S (no Antarctica). It looks like the maps everyone knows without
inflating high latitudes as much as Mercator. The formula is in `tools/pipeline/comun.py` and in
`scripts/nucleo/proyeccion.gd`; if it changes, the data is regenerated.

## Code structure

```
scripts/
  principal.gd          entry point: loading screen, sets up map and interface, automatic screenshots
  nucleo/               global state, map data, encyclopedia, Wikipedia, projection, dates
  mapa/                 camera view, names layer, markers layer, Pleiades places layer
  interfaz/             theme, top bar, timeline, side panel, legend, settings, welcome
shaders/                map, vignette and Pleiades dots
data/mapa/              data generated by the pipeline (Godot doesn't import it: .gdignore)
data/enciclopedia/      encyclopedia JSON packages, their schema and the copies of the reading links
data/demografia/        Our World in Data series by country and present-day country of each map unit
data/pleiades/          Pleiades ancient places and their join with the encyclopedia's places
tools/pipeline/         Python: physical base, units and borders, Wikidata, preview
tools/enciclopedia/     package validation, coverage, population, copies of the links, compact format
tools/pleiades/         Python: Pleiades places and their join with the encyclopedia (Wikidata)
raw_data/               original sources (GEBCO, Natural Earth, Blue Marble)
legado/                 the first attempt with QGIS, as a reference
```

The interface is built in code (not in editor scenes) so that visual themes are just palettes and can be switched
live.

## Quality options and views

- **Quality** (Settings): low, medium, high and ultra. They change texture resolution, relief, the number of border
  samples, inner glow, waves, paper and animated water. "Low" uses a quarter of the video memory.
- **Interface themes:** Marble and bronze, Illuminated parchment, and Ink and brass. Each one is a color dictionary
  in `scripts/interfaz/tema.gd`; adding another is a matter of copying one.
- **3D view** (planned): a mesh with the real relief (elevation is already in the pipeline), the same political
  texture from the 2D view projected on top, water with its own shader, clouds and particles. It'll be selectable in
  Settings, with Forward+ (the default renderer on Linux and Windows, with a fallback to OpenGL for older machines;
  on Mac the default is Compatibility, and anyone can choose the renderer in Settings).

## How it's tested

- `godot --headless --path . -- --prueba`: runs through the app unattended (year jumps, modes, selection and tabs in
  several places around the world, every entry, searches, qualities and themes) and writes any script error to the
  output. It ends with "TERMINADA sin cortes".
- `tools/captura.sh salida.png --anio=-1350 --lon=31 --lat=27.6 --zoom=1.2 --seleccionar --pestana=2`: takes a real
  screenshot without a monitor (with the `cage` compositor), useful for reviewing the look and for the README.
- `python3 tools/enciclopedia/validar.py`: validates the encyclopedia packages.
- `tools/godot/tmap.py`: the bridge for driving the app and the editor from outside (screenshots, year, camera,
  clicks, errors, GDScript); see [tools/godot/README.en.md](../../tools/godot/README.en.md). `tmap.py revisar` compiles
  all scripts without opening the app.
- `python3 tools/pipeline/lamina.py ANIO x0 y0 x1 y1 salida.png`: a high-quality static plate of any year, without
  Godot (for example, [the world in AD 117](../assets/mundo_117.png)).

## Storage and downloads

TellMAP stores entries and links to the texts: what it distributes is measured in tens or hundreds of MB, not
terabytes. The bulky stuff (people from Wikidata, population grids) will be downloaded in tiles by era and region, on
demand, with a download manager that never downloads anything while the timeline is moving and a capped cache. The
plan, the thresholds and where each thing is hosted (GitHub releases, with mirrors on Internet Archive and Zenodo;
not Google Drive) are in [Storage and downloads](storage.md).

## Performance measured in the pipeline

- 58,076 units, 1,001,996 events, 43 snapshots and 13,272 labels.
- Map data on disk: ~26 MB (terrain 1.7 MB in WebP, relief 16.7 MB, units 2 MB, politics 6 MB).
- In video memory, at high quality: ~320 MB (terrain and relief with mipmaps, units). At low quality, ~110 MB.
